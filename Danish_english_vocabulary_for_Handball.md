# Danish for Handball

*Samuel's phrasebook: court, training and team life in Denmark*

> Practise these phrases with audio and pronunciation checks in the
> [Handball Dansk trainer](https://fzaire.github.io/danish-english-handball-language/).
> Also available as [PDF](https://fzaire.github.io/danish-english-handball-language/docs/Danish_english_vocabulary_for_Handball.pdf) and [web page](https://fzaire.github.io/danish-english-handball-language/docs/Danish_english_vocabulary_for_Handball.html).

Pronunciation hints are approximate (English-based). Danish swallows word endings and has a soft "r", so listening matters more than spelling. Danes switch to English quickly, so Samuel should say "Du må gerne tale dansk til mig" ("You may speak Danish to me") early on.

## Week 1 priority order

Sections 7 (coach shouts) → 3 (positions) → 4 (game actions) → 1 (essentials) → 9 (logistics). Sections 5 and 6 follow once he recognises the coach's rhythm. Coaches speak in short fragments, so the commands and positions cover about 80% of what he will hear in practice.

## 12. Pronunciation traps

- **Soft D:** the d after vowels is like a soft English "th" in mad, rød, med, blad.
- **Vowels æ, ø, å:** æ ≈ "air" (short), ø ≈ "uh" with rounded lips, å ≈ "aw".
- **Silent endings:** -et and -ede are swallowed, so spillede ("played") sounds like "spell-uh".
- **Stød (glottal catch):** a throat catch that changes meaning (hun "she" vs hund "dog"). Learned by ear.
- **"Jeg":** pronounced "yai".
- **Sentence rhythm:** flat and fast. Learn the commands (section 7) and positions (section 3) first.
